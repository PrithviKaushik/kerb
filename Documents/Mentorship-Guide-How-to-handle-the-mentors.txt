Use this document whenever advice regarding mentorship or per-round deliverables is asked:

**Mentorship Guide**  
*Use the mentors. Don’t just perform for them.*

---

### Core Mindset

Round 1 and Round 2 exist to make the product better in the next 16 hours.

Being remembered is a side-effect of being clear, specific, and responsive.  
Confidence comes from having already reasoned through the hard parts — it is not the same as refusing to update your mind.

Treat every mentoring session as the highest-bandwidth debugging resource available.  
Defend positions you have already thought through. Change positions that turn out to be weaker than you believed.

---

### Technical Position (use this, don’t force it)

**Primary line:**

> “Most systems try to answer: *Was this a violation?*  
> Kerb answers a more useful question:  
> *Here is the evidence, here is how much we trust it, and here is where the camera cannot support a reliable four-tire decision.*  
> The steward always stays in control.”

Supporting concrete language:
- Contact-region hierarchy (Level 1 → Level 2 proxy → UNCERTAIN)
- Sporting rule vs 3-of-5 reliability filter
- Explicit refusal to invent invisible tires
- Structured evidence package that always ends with **STEWARD REVIEW REQUIRED**

These phrases signal maturity. Use them when they fit the conversation, not as slogans.

### Round 1 (5:00–6:30 PM today) — 7–10 minutes

**Goal:**  
Show that the foundation is real and the direction is deliberate. Leave with usable feedback.

#### 6-Slide Structure

1. **Title + Positioning**  
   Kerb – Evidence-grade steward assistant (not an automatic referee)

2. **Problem & Our Reading**  
   Organizer requirements mapped to engineering interpretation

3. **System Architecture**  
   Clean pipeline: Video → YOLO → ByteTrack → Contact Approximation → Boundary → 4-Outside Rule → Temporal Filter → Trust → Evidence

4. **Key Technical Decisions**  
   Contact hierarchy + Temporal rule + simple UML-style relationships  
   Safety rule highlighted: never treat unobservable as outside

5. **Differentiation + Intended Demo Cases**  
   Positioning line + four cases (clean / clear candidate / near-line / uncertain)

6. **Current Status + What We Want Feedback On**  
   Honest progress + specific open questions

#### Conversation Beats (flexible order)

Hit these in whatever sequence the conversation allows:

- Clear positioning (helper, not referee)
- Architecture and why the layers are separated
- Contact hierarchy and the UNCERTAIN path
- Temporal filter as reliability mechanism, not sporting rule
- The four intended demo cases
- Current real status (what actually runs right now)
- Genuine open questions (do not leave these only for the end)

**Strong open questions (prepare 1–2):**
- “Where do you see the biggest risk in the contact approximation approach?”
- “Does treating uncertainty as a first-class output feel correct for a steward tool?”
- “Any strong view on majority window versus consecutive frames for the reliability filter?”
- “What would make this evidence package actually useful under real time pressure?”

#### Defend vs Act-On Rule (critical)

| Type | Signal | Response |
|------|--------|----------|
| **Defend** | Mentor questions a deliberate, already-reasoned tradeoff | Calmly restate the reasoning. Use prepared logic. |
| **Act on** | Mentor surfaces a real gap, better framing, missing failure mode, or practical weakness | Acknowledge it. Write it down. Say “That’s useful — we’ll take that into the overnight work.” |

When a locked decision is challenged:
1. Briefly restate why it was locked.
2. Ask one clarifying question.
3. If their point is stronger → unlock it and note the change.
4. If not → hold the line cleanly and move on.

Do not default to pure defense.  
Do not collapse into agreement on everything.

**Approximations:**  
Defend deliberate MVP scoping choices without apology.  
If a mentor flags an actual gap in reasoning, the correct move is “You’re right, let us fix that.”

#### Feedback Capture
One person writes exact phrases where possible.  
Immediately after the session (5–10 min):
- Must act on before Round 2
- Nice to have
- Disagree / park

### Round 2 (09:00–10:30 AM) — 10 minutes

**Goal:**  
Show you listened and the product moved. Get final high-value input before the noon freeze.

#### Opening (choose the true version)

**If feedback was clear and attributable:**
> “Yesterday you raised [specific point]. Here’s exactly what we changed and what it looks like now.”

**If feedback was vague, mixed, or light:**
> “We took the discussion on [topic] and pushed harder on [specific improvement]. Here’s the current state of the MVP.”

Do not invent a clean narrative that isn’t true.

#### Structure
- Short, honest mapping of what changed (or what was hardened)
- Demo the strongest cases (include the uncertain case)
- Point at the concrete improvements
- One or two focused questions for final advice
- Stop and listen

---

### Demonstration Tips (both rounds)

- A real running pipeline (even rough) beats perfect slides.
- When showing the Uncertain case, frame it as discipline: “This is the system correctly refusing to guess.”
- If live inference is risky, use pre-rendered evidence packages and state it cleanly.
- Speak slightly slower than feels natural. Precision > speed.
- If interrupted, answer the question, then return to the nearest useful beat. There is no need to finish a script.

---

### FAQ Anchors (for reasoned defense)

- **Four tires:** Hierarchy + explicit UNCERTAIN. We never invent.
- **Bounding box:** Useful for tracking, invalid as tire locations. Used only as Level-2 proxy and labelled approximate.
- **3-of-5:** Reliability filter only. Sporting rule remains all-four-outside.
- **Penalties:** Never. Every output ends with STEWARD REVIEW REQUIRED.
- **Scope:** Single-camera vertical slice by design. Generalization is future work.

**Final Principle**

Walk in clear and specific.  
Use the mentors as the best debugging tool you will get.  
Defend what you have already reasoned through.  
Change what turns out to be weaker.  
Then show the difference in Round 2.

That combination produces both a better product and a team people actually remember.
